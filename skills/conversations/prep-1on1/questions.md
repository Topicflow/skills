# Question bank — prep-1on1

Real questions, per lane and chair, from the sources the library trusts. Use them as raw
material: tie a question to something real in this person's week, never paste one bare. Sources:
Rogelberg (*Glad We Met*; HBR's "28 Questions to Ask Your Boss"), Manager Tools (10/10/10),
GitLab handbook, Lara Hogan, Culture Amp, Lattice, Fellow, Julie Zhuo, Mark Rabkin, Wes Kao.

## The person — how they are actually doing

From the manager's chair:

- "Think about what's going on in life and at work — how are things for you, say 0 to 10?"
  (Rogelberg; the scale makes "fine" harder to hide behind)
- "What's top of mind for you right now?" (Zhuo)
- "Last time you said X was weighing on you — how is that going?" (Culture Amp; requires notes)
- "How's life outside work?" (Fellow — ask it and mean it)
- "What's making you grumpy lately, and how would I know?" (Hogan)

From the report's chair, about the relationship and the manager:

- "What are your priorities over the next few weeks — and what can I take off your plate?"
  (Rogelberg's 28 questions; support flows up too)
- "Is there anything keeping you up at night that I can help with?" (same)

## The work — blockers and decisions, not status

Either chair — the shape matters more than the words:

- "What's the best use of our time today?" (Zhuo)
- "What's hard for you in getting to that outcome?" / "What's the worst case you're worried
  about?" (Zhuo)
- "What roadblocks do you need help with?" (Lattice)
- Report's chair, the Kao shape: recommendation first, response wanted named — "I recommend X.
  I need a decision / direction / just support." Pure updates go in writing before the meeting,
  not on the agenda.
- Report's chair, priorities: "Given what's on my plate, what should I prioritize — and can you
  help me understand why?" (Rogelberg's 28)

## The direction — growth, career, feedback both ways

Career, manager's chair:

- "When you think about yourself in two years, what comes to mind?" (Culture Amp)
- "What two or three skills would you like to build on the job?" (Culture Amp)
- "What would you like to own next?" (Lattice)

Career, report's chair:

- "What can I do now to prepare for the next step?" / "What strengths do you see in me, and
  where could they take me?" (Rogelberg's 28)
- "How does my work fit into the broader goals — what's going on further up that I should
  know?" (same)

Feedback, both directions — forced-choice beats open-ended (Rabkin):

- "What should I start, stop, or continue doing?" (either chair)
- "What do you wish I did more of — less of?" (Rabkin)
- Manager asking: "Do you want more or less direction from me right now?" (Fellow)
- Report asking: "What feedback do you have on how I handled X?" — name the X (Rogelberg's 28)

## Closers

- "What are you committing to between now and next time — and what am I?" (Culture Amp; P4
  wants owners on both sides)
- "What was the most useful part of this conversation?" (Zhuo; tunes the next agenda)

## What the sources agree on

The report talks 50-90% of the time (Rogelberg; GitLab says 80). Status belongs in writing, not
in the meeting (Kao; Rogelberg). If every 1-on-1 feels comfortable, the real topics are being
avoided (Rabkin, Zhuo). And the meetings rated most valuable are the ones where the report built
the agenda (Rogelberg) — which is why the report's chair of this skill elicits instead of
handing over a finished list.
